# Cement and the Kendeng Mountains

*Heinrich Böll Foundation Southeast Asia*

## The Core Conflict

The Kendeng Mountains in Central Java, Indonesia face destruction from limestone mining operations intended to produce cement. Local communities, particularly the indigenous Sedulur Sikep (also called Samin or Wong Sikep) have mounted sustained resistance since 2006.

## Environmental and Social Impact

### Flooding Crisis

The region has experienced intensified flooding since 1998. One resident, Ali (nicknamed Nopet), describes how "almost every year floods always occur...water reaches waist height."

### Water and Agricultural Threats

Farmer Bambang notes the karst landscape contains "many springs that will be lost if the mountain is mined," threatening food security and livelihoods dependent on productive agricultural land.

## The Corporate Players

German company HeidelbergCement holds majority stakes in Indocement (51%), making it a major player in Indonesia's cement market alongside state-owned Semen Indonesia and Holcim Indonesia.

## Community Response

The Sedulur Sikep movement represents one of Southeast Asia's longest peasant-based resistance efforts. Farmer Gunarti exemplifies this resolve, stating her commitment to preserving ancestral lands for future generations despite corporate pressure.

## Documentation and Support

The Heinrich Böll Foundation has documented this struggle through articles, graphic novels, documentaries, and international solidarity campaigns connecting Indonesian communities with German activists opposed to corporate environmental destruction.

## Transnational Solidarity

The Save Kendeng network connects Indonesian grassroots resistance with international environmental and human rights organizations, highlighting the global dimensions of local resource conflicts.
