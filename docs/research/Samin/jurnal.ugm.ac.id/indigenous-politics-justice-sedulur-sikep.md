# The Indigenous Politics of Justice: the Case of the Sedulur Sikep Movement in Central Java

**Authors:** Ronald Adam and Zainal Abidin Bagir
*Center for Religious and Cross-Cultural Studies, Universitas Gadjah Mada, Yogyakarta*

**Journal:** Jurnal Kawistara, Vol. 12, No. 2 (2022)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.22146/kawistara.78485

## Abstract Summary

The research examines how indigenous communities resist capitalist expansion through their land defense efforts. The authors argue that scholarly analyses often artificially separate "cultural/spiritual" dimensions from "political-economic" ones, missing crucial complexity in actual resistance movements.

## Key Findings

### First Major Finding

The researchers discovered that Wong Sikep people unite spiritual and material concerns. As the abstract states, their agricultural system reflects both "the teachings of their ancestors (culture/spirituality) as well as their practical needs (political economy)."

### Second Major Finding

This integration shapes their resistance strategy, where they simultaneously pursue "both the politics of recognition and redistribution in their resistance to cement mining."

## Methodology

The study involved qualitative fieldwork including:
- Four months of observations with Wong Sikep households
- Interviews with twenty individuals from fifteen households
- Location: Baturejo Sukolilo Village, Pati Regency, Central Java

## Keywords

Indigenous Movements, Politics of Justice, Politics of Recognition, Politics of Redistribution, Social Movement Theory

## Significance

This academic study provides scholarly analysis of how the Sedulur Sikep movement integrates cultural identity with economic justice concerns in their resistance to industrial expansion (cement mining) in the Kendeng Mountains region.
