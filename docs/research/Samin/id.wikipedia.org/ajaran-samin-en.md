# Samin Teachings

**Samin Teachings** (also called the **Samin Movement** or **Saminism**) is one of the belief systems found on the island of Java, Indonesia.

## Background

Samin Surosentiko was a famous figure who taught **sedulur sikep** (sincere siblings), where followers ignited the spirit of resistance against the Dutch in forms other than violence. The forms of resistance included refusing to pay taxes and rejecting all regulations made by the colonial government. This community often troubled both the Dutch colonial government and the Japanese occupation because of this attitude.

The Samin community also isolated themselves to the extent that only in the 1970s did they learn that Indonesia had become independent.

## Distribution

The Samin groups are spread throughout Central Java, but their greatest concentration is in the **Blora** and **Bojonegoro** areas, residing at the border between Central Java and East Java. Their numbers are small and they live in the Kendeng mountain region.

The Samin groups prefer to be called **wong sikep** (people of integrity), because the word "samin" carries negative connotations for them.

## Founder

The core teachings of Samin Surosentiko, whose birth name was **Raden Kohar**, born in Ploso Kedhiren Village, Randublatung, in 1859, and died while exiled in Padang, 1914.

## Five Teachings of Samin Followers

1. Study at home (do not attend outside schools)
2. Do not wear a peci (cap), but wear an "iket" (cloth tied around the head)
3. Do not wear long pants, only wear knee-length pants
4. Rejection of capitalism

## Spread of the Movement

First spread in the Klopoduwur area, Blora. In 1890, the Samin movement developed in two forest villages in the Randublatung area, Blora. The movement then quickly spread to other villages, from the north coast of Java to the forests in the North and South Kendeng Mountains.

## Core Principles of Saminism

### Religious Principle

**Religion** is a weapon or guide for life. Samin philosophy does not differentiate between religions, therefore Samin people never deny or hate religion. What matters is one's **character** in life.

### Behavioral Principles

- Do not disturb others, do not quarrel, do not be envious, and do not take others' belongings
- Be patient and do not be arrogant
- Humans must understand their life because life is the same as spirit and there is only one, carried eternally forever

### Speech Principles

When speaking, one must guard one's mouth, be honest, and show mutual respect. **Trading is forbidden for Samin people** because trade contains elements of "dishonesty." Also, one must not accept donations in the form of money.

## Sacred Texts

The Samin people have a "sacred text" called **Serat Jamus Kalimasada** which consists of several books:

- Serat Punjer Kawitan (Book of Origins)
- Serat Pikukuh Kasajaten (Book of True Life Foundations)
- Serat Uri-uri Pambudi (Book of Cultivating Wisdom)
- Serat Jati Sawit (Book of True Identity)
- Serat Lampahing Urip (Book of Life's Journey)

The teachings in the book Serat Pikukuh Kasajaten (establishment of true life) are written in the form of tembang poetry, a traditional poetic genre in Javanese literature.

## Life Principles

By following this sacred text, the Samin people seek to build an **inner realm** free from attitudes of **drengki srei, tukar padu, dahpen kemeren** (envy, quarreling, jealousy). Instead, they wish to fulfill the command:

> "Practice patience and effort. Remember to be patient. Practice effort."

## Language and Clothing

### Language

They do not recognize levels of Javanese language, so the language used is **Javanese ngoko** (informal Javanese). For them, respecting others is not shown through the language used but through attitudes and actions demonstrated.

### Clothing

- **Men**: Long-sleeved shirt without collar, black in color, wearing a headcloth
- **Women**: Long-sleeved kebaya, with cloth reaching below the kneecap or above the ankle

## Marriage

According to Samin teachings, **marriage** is very important. In their teachings, marriage is a tool for achieving nobility of character which in turn creates "**Atmaja Tama**" (noble children).

In Samin teachings, the groom is required to recite a declaration:

> "Since the Prophet Adam, my work has indeed been marriage. (This time) marrying a woman named...... I promise to be faithful to her. We have lived together as two."

According to Samin people, a marriage is considered valid even if only the parents of the bride and groom officiate.

## Attitude Toward the Environment

The Samin community's view of the environment is very positive. They utilize nature (for example, taking wood) only as needed and never exploit it.

**Land** for them is like their own mother, meaning land gives them livelihood. As traditional farmers, they treat their land as well as possible.

The Samin community understands that nature's contents and wealth will be depleted or not depending on those who use it.

## Settlement

Samin community settlements usually cluster in a row of houses to facilitate communication. These houses are made of wood, especially teak, and also bamboo. The house buildings are relatively spacious with **limasan**, **kampung**, or **joglo** architectural styles.

## Traditions

Traditional ceremonies of the Samin community include **nyadran** (village cleansing) which also involves draining an old well that has provided many benefits to the community. Life cycle ceremonies related to pregnancy, birth, circumcision, marriage, and death. They perform these traditions simply.

## The Samin Community Today

Changing times have also influenced Samin community traditions. They now use **tractors** and **chemical fertilizers** in agriculture, as well as household equipment made of plastic, aluminum, and other materials.
