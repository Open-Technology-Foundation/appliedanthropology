# Origins of the Samin Tribe and Its Development in Bojonegoro

## History and Origins

The Samin Tribe community originates from the teachings of **Saminism** founded by Samin Surosentiko in Ploso Kedhiren Village, Blora. These teachings emerged around the 1890s as a response to indigenous resistance against the Dutch colonial system, particularly related to tax obligations and land exploitation.

Due to their strong resistance attitude, the Samin community withdrew from broader social life. "Until the 1970s, some Samin community groups only learned that Indonesia had become independent."

Over time, many Samin community members migrated to Bojonegoro and the Kendeng Mountain region stretching between Central Java and East Java.

## Core Teaching: Sedulur Sikep

The core life principle is **Sedulur Sikep**, meaning "good and honest people." This philosophy emphasizes honesty, simplicity, and straightforward character.

### Six Ethical Principles of Prohibition

| Principle | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **Drengki** | Slander |
| **Srei** | Greed |
| **Panasten** | Easily offended |
| **Dawen** | Accusing without evidence |
| **Kemeren** | Envy |
| **Nyiyo Marang Sepodo** | Committing vile acts against fellow humans |

### Five Interaction Prohibitions

| Prohibition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| **Bedok** | Accusing |
| **Colong** | Stealing |
| **Penthil** | Taking items from nature |
| **Jumput** | Taking market goods |
| **Nemu wae ora keno** | Prohibition on taking found items |

Their fundamental principle: "one must not waste fellow human beings, whoever they are, as long as they want to be considered siblings."

## Daily Life and Beliefs

The Samin community uses plain Javanese (ngoko alus) in daily communication. They follow the **Religion of Adam** (Agama Adam), a belief practiced every morning and at dusk.

They honor nature as the figure of "mother" (biyung) who must be protected and not damaged. Their traditional house, **Rumah Bekuk Lulang**, reflects simplicity and closeness to the environment.

## Attitude Toward Modernization

Despite maintaining strong traditions, the Samin community does not reject modern developments. They are "open to technological and educational developments," utilizing electricity, computers, and electronic devices to access agricultural information.

They also consider formal education as a path to a better life, while still maintaining core values such as honesty, tolerance, togetherness, and mutual cooperation (gotong royong).

## Traditional Cuisine

The iconic food of the Samin community is **nasi tiwul**, processed from peeled and dried cassava into rice-like grains. This dish is served with seasoned tofu (baceman tahu), tempeh, vegetable salad (urap), salted fish, and onion sambal—a simple yet highly nutritious combination.
