# Why the Community of Sedulur Sikep and Kendeng Want to Preserve the Environment

*Friedrich Naumann Foundation for Freedom - Indonesia*

## Core Philosophy

The Sedulur Sikep community, rooted in Samin Surosentiko's 19th-20th century belief system, views the earth as sacred rather than a commodity. Their foundational principle holds that land belongs to the Divine and ancestors, not to be exploited for profit.

As expressed by environmental activist Gunarti: **"Love mother earth as we love ourselves."** This philosophy extends beyond humans—the community believes environmental tolerance is equally important as human tolerance.

## Environmental Concerns

The primary threat comes from proposed cement factory construction in the Kendeng Mountains. The region, spanning Pati, Rembang, Grobogan, and Blora, contains karst geology and provides critical water sources.

### Key Environmental Assets at Risk

- **49 identified freshwater springs** flowing through underground caves and streams
- Diverse flora and fauna dependent on the ecosystem
- Cool microclimate created by mountain forests
- Agricultural land supporting traditional farming practices

## Resistance Strategy

The community organizes through nonviolent methods, reflecting their teaching to "improve behaviour and always tell the truth." Women play central roles, with Gunarti leading educational initiatives and organizing village-to-village awareness campaigns.

### Cross-Religious Unity

Groups like the **Women's Environmental Care Group (KPPL) Simbar Wareh** unite people across religious lines—Muslim, Christian, and traditional believers collaborate despite sectarian differences.

## Motivation for Preservation

Protection stems from understanding that industrial development would:

- Eliminate livelihood sources
- Destroy water access
- Erase cultural heritage
- Benefit external interests rather than local communities

## The Sedulur Sikep Teaching

The community's environmental ethic derives directly from Samin Surosentiko's teachings about living harmoniously with nature, treating the earth as a mother figure (biyung) that sustains all life and must be protected for future generations.
