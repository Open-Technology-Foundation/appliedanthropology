# Historical Roots of Saminism and the Philosophical Teachings of Samin Surosentiko

**By Galih Pranata**
*Editor: Mahandis Yoanata Thamrin*
*National Geographic Indonesia*

## Background

Samin Surosentiko (born 1859 in Klopo Duwur, Blora) initiated a people's movement against the practices of colonialism and imperialism that harmed the Blora community. This activism began when the Boschwezen (Dutch East Indies Forestry Service) restricted community access to forests, particularly Randoeblatoeng, to monopolize the sale of valuable teak wood.

## Catalyst for Resistance

When the Dutch established *houtvesterijen* (forest management zones) and imposed heavy taxes, local farmers lost traditional access to resources and land use rights. Samin responded by continuing to harvest wood despite colonial prohibitions, resulting in repeated imprisonment.

## Philosophical Foundation

According to Restu Trinova's 2010 comparative study in the Philosophy Journal, "Saminism taught the essence of human freedom." Core principles included:

- Rejecting the desire to possess others' property
- Advocating for human equality
- Refusing symbolic submission to colonial authority
- Opposing taxation and hierarchical structures

## Social Expression

Followers distinguished themselves through linguistic choices—using only casual Javanese (*ngoko*)—and distinctive collarless black long-sleeved shirts. By 1906, the movement had spread to Pati, Madiun, Grobogan, Bojonegoro, and Tuban.

## Suppression and Legacy

Worried about increasing support, Dutch authorities banned Saminism and exiled Samin to Padang until his death. Modern adherents prefer to call themselves *Sedulur Sikep* (sincere siblings), because "Samin" has acquired negative connotations in mainstream discourse.
