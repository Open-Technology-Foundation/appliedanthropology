# Dharma and Science

**By Prof. P. L. Dhar**

## Introduction

Human curiosity drives us to understand our world through two complementary pathways. Science systematizes knowledge about the external world through sensory observation, while Dharma organizes wisdom about inner understanding and life's purpose. As the Isa-Upanishad suggests, combining spiritual wisdom with secular knowledge enables us to transcend mortality while maintaining physical wellbeing.

## Misunderstandings About Dharma

Contemporary confusion conflates Dharma with sectarian religion, rituals, and dogmatic thinking. This perception prevents rational people from engaging with its principles. Yet authentic Dharma operates as applied science—testable, verifiable, and free from supernatural claims. The Buddha exemplified this approach, urging followers to "believe nothing merely because told it" but to verify teachings through personal experience.

## Dharma as Applied Science

Dharma's essence involves ethical conduct, mental purification, and insight development. These aren't mystical but practical—observable through disciplined practice. The mind resembles turbid water; purification requires identifying and eliminating mental defilements rather than merely suppressing them.

## Vipassana: The Core Practice

Vipassana meditation represents Dharma's practical application. Practitioners observe bodily sensations objectively, gradually dissolving habitual mental patterns. The preliminary practice, Anapana, cultivates concentration through mindful breathing observation—non-sectarian, readily accessible, and neutral.

Moral precepts provide essential groundwork, making objective self-observation possible. This transforms morality from external obligation into experiential understanding.

## Modern Science's Paradigm Shift

Nineteenth-century materialism claimed matter as sole reality. Contemporary discoveries challenge this worldview fundamentally:

**Fundamental Particles:** Matter consists of dynamic energy patterns rather than solid objects. Particles exhibit both wave and particle characteristics depending on observation conditions.

**Quantum Mechanics:** The observer influences observed properties—mind shapes physical reality. This validates ancient insights about consciousness's role in experience.

**Complementarity Principle:** Apparently contradictory evidence represents different aspects of singular reality. Wisdom integrates seemingly opposite perspectives rather than insisting on exclusive truth.

## Implications for Understanding Reality

Modern biology reveals profound impermanence: approximately 98 percent of human body atoms replace annually. Individual identity persists as abstract pattern rather than material substance—supporting the Buddhist concept of anatta (egolessness).

As Walt Whitman poetically expressed: "Every atom belonging to me as good belongs to you"—now scientifically validated. Molecular biology similarly demonstrates that genetic uniqueness resides in non-material pattern arrangements.

## Convergence of Perspectives

Recent physics increasingly describes a fundamental quantum field underlying physical phenomena. This impersonal field resembles transcendent reality described by Eastern contemplatives. The television analogy illustrates how consciousness might similarly arise from this underlying field.

## Conclusion

Both domains reveal universal laws: science addresses external phenomena while Dharma illuminates inner reality. Their integration requires developing intuitive capacities beyond basic sensory perception.

Paraphrasing Einstein's insight: "Science without Dharma lacks vision; Dharma without Science lacks power." Humanity urgently needs channeling scientific capabilities through ethical wisdom—reorienting life through moral discipline, mental cultivation, and progressive purification via Vipassana practice.
