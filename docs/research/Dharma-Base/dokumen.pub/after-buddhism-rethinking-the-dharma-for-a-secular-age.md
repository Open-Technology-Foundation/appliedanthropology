# After Buddhism: Rethinking the Dharma for a Secular Age

## Document Overview

This is an excerpt from Stephen Batchelor's 2015 book published by Yale University Press. The text includes the preface, Chapter 1 ("After Buddhism"), and Chapter 2 ("Mahānāma: The Convert").

## Key Themes

**Batchelor's Core Argument**

Batchelor proposes returning to early Buddhist sources to develop a secular, modern interpretation of dharma practice. He argues that the Buddha taught pragmatically rather than metaphysically, focusing on reducing suffering through direct experience rather than accepting dogmatic beliefs about rebirth or cosmic truths.

**The Four Central Principles**

Batchelor identifies what he calls the "four P's" as Buddhism's distinctive contribution:

- Conditionality (understanding how things depend on causes and conditions)
- A fourfold task (comprehending suffering, releasing reactivity, observing cessation, cultivating the eightfold path)
- Mindful awareness perspective
- Self-reliance in practicing dharma

**Emptiness Reconceptualized**

Rather than viewing emptiness as ultimate metaphysical truth, Batchelor interprets it as "a way of dwelling on earth unconditioned by reactivity." He emphasizes practical sensibility over abstract philosophical understanding, drawing on early Pali Canon sources that suggest "to dwell in emptiness" means inhabiting embodied experience without habitual reactivity patterns.

## Mahānāma's Story

Chapter 2 contextualizes Buddhist practice through Gotama's cousin, who remained a householder rather than becoming a mendicant. Mahānāma served as chief of the Sakiyan clan while practicing dharma in the secular world, exemplifying how Buddhist principles apply beyond monastic life.
