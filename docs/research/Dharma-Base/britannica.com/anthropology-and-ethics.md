# Anthropology and Ethics

## Overview

The relationship between anthropological study and ethical philosophy centers on understanding how moral systems vary across cultures while questioning whether objective moral principles exist.

## Moral Universals vs. Cultural Variation

### Common Ground Across Societies

Despite significant differences in how moral principles are applied, certain fundamentals appear nearly universal. "Concern for kin and reciprocity are considered good in virtually all human societies." Additionally, all cultures maintain restrictions on killing and harming their own members.

### Historical Perspective on Variation

The ancient Greek historian Herodotus documented striking differences in practices. He recorded how Persian King Darius I discovered that Greeks refused to consume their deceased fathers' bodies while some Indian groups found the opposite practice—cremation of fathers—equally abhorrent. This observation led Herodotus to conclude that each society considers its own customs superior.

### Systematic Study of Moral Diversity

Finnish anthropologist Edward Westermarck conducted the first comprehensive examination in his work *The Origin and Development of the Moral Ideas* (1906–08). He documented variations across societies regarding killing, familial duties, sexuality, women's status, property rights, slavery, truthfulness, dietary rules, and animal treatment. His research confirmed that flourishing human societies maintain radically different moral viewpoints.

## Ethics vs. Anthropological Description

A crucial distinction exists: anthropology *describes* moral systems, while ethics *justifies* them. The discipline of ethics must acknowledge cultural variation but recognizes that descriptive differences don't establish moral relativism.

## The Relativism Question

Some argue that moral diversity proves relativism—that no universal principles exist. However, this reasoning contains logical flaws. Universal agreement doesn't establish moral truth, just as widespread disagreement doesn't disprove objective standards. Historical societies might simply have been morally mistaken.

Human uncertainty about proper conduct isn't resolved by learning what one's society endorses or discovering widespread agreement rooted in evolutionary advantage.
