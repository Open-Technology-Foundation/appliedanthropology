# The Dharmic Ideal Of Governance–A Contemporary Perspective

**Authors:** M.S. Srinivasan and O.P. Dani

---

## The Ancient and Modern Ideal of Dharma

The article examines how political systems emerge from particular interpretations of Dharma—understood as the Laws of Life or Nature. Ancient Indian civilization perceived "a moral Law and Order...governing life in Man, Nature and the Universe," emphasizing character development and individual contribution to collective good based on natural capacity.

Modern systems, by contrast, developed from scientific readings of nature. Early capitalism adopted Darwinian principles of survival and competition, creating societies where technological efficiency and specialist classes dominate. However, contemporary ecology reveals nature operates through cooperation and partnership—principles more aligned with ancient Indian thought.

## The Human Face of Governance

Indian governance rests on four foundational principles:

**The Ideal of Dharma** represents ideas and values harmonizing with higher laws, promoting human development.

**Four Human Types:**
- Mentor (intellectual/ethical pursuits)
- Marshal (will and vital force)
- Merchant (emotional/pragmatic concerns)
- Worker (physical labor)

**Swadharma** means each person should perform work matching their nature, fostering both individual growth and collective excellence.

**Social Organization** reflects inner psychological hierarchies, with spiritual consciousness providing overarching guidance to mentors and marshals, who lead culture and polity respectively.

## The Aim of Government

Government ensures each social organ functions according to its dharma in harmonious balance. The sovereign only intervenes when dharma deviates or injustice occurs. Development encompasses both individual spiritual progress and community material wellbeing—not merely economic metrics.

## Lessons for the Present

Contemporary governance prioritizes economic development (GDP, per capita income) without addressing inner human development. The Indian paradigm offers an alternative emphasizing education, character-building, and spiritual growth.

The approach suggests organizing societal psychological energies toward constructive activities by clarifying each group's social dharma. Currently, merchants pursue corporate dominance over honest wealth creation; marshals emphasize economic development over justice; mentors focus on utilitarian values rather than cultural elevation.

A balanced society requires government motivation of creative energies aligned with respective swadharmas, achieving "balanced development...with minimum wastage and maximum efficiency."
