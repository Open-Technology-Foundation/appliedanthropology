# Pursuing 'Dharma in DNA': Oxford-published author explores the connections between science and Buddhism

**By Hannah Ashton**

## Overview

Dee Denver, an evolutionary biologist and Head of the Department of Integrative Biology at Oregon State, has published a book exploring how Buddhism and biology intersect. His work challenges Western assumptions about both disciplines.

## The Book's Premise

Denver's first book, *The Dharma in DNA: Insights at the Intersection of Biology and Buddhism*, was published by Oxford University Press and ranked as the No. 1 New Release in Buddhism on Amazon in April and May.

The author explains that Buddhism "is logical. It's a tradition that values logic and reason," though it differs from science in some areas. He notes: "I would say both sides have rational criticisms of the other side, but it's productive."

## Three Core Buddhist Principles Examined

Denver tests three Buddhist concepts against biological evidence:

1. **Anitya** (impermanence)
2. **Anatman** (absence of permanent self)
3. **Pratityasamutpada** (interconnected cause-and-effect)

### DNA and Mutual Inheritance

His research discovered that 63% of women who bore sons harbored their son's Y chromosome DNA in multiple brain regions. Denver argues this demonstrates that inheritance isn't strictly one-directional, supporting the Buddhist principle of mutual, interconnected relationships.

## "Bodhi Science" Framework

Denver proposes incorporating Buddhist wisdom—specifically selflessness, detachment, awareness, and compassion—as an ethical foundation for scientific practice. He contends this approach could prevent pseudoscience while enhancing scientific discovery through mindfulness.

## Personal Connection: Adoption

Denver and his wife adopted two children from Ethiopia, embodying the Buddhist principle of *bodhicitta* (universal compassion). He reflects that "my love and compassion are equally available to anyone and everyone, regardless of the inheritance paths of DNA."
