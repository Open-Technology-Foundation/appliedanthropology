# Humanizing Evolution: Anthropology, the Evolutionary Synthesis, and the Prehistory of Biological Anthropology, 1927–1962

**Author:** Vassiliki Betty Smocovitis
**Published in:** *Current Anthropology*, Vol. 53, No. S5

## Overview

This paper explores the various attempts to integrate anthropology—and anthropologists—within the wider synthesis of evolution in the interval of time between 1927 and 1962 by tracking intersecting individuals and groupings at critical junctures such as conferences, commemorative events, and collaborative publications.

## Focus and Methodology

The author focuses on the discipline as a unit of historical analysis and on a series of rhetorical arguments used to discipline and bound areas of study that grounded the secular philosophy of **evolutionary humanism**.

She traces the beginnings of an originary narrative and offers a kind of prehistory of what was first referred to as "human evolution" and then "biological anthropology"—an area of study that brought humans into the discipline of evolutionary biology.

## Key Figures Examined

The paper examines the key roles played by "architects" of the evolutionary synthesis and their relations with anthropologists:

### Architects of the Evolutionary Synthesis
- **Theodosius Dobzhansky**
- **Julian Huxley**
- **G. G. Simpson**
- **Ernst Mayr**

### Prominent Anthropologists
- **Sherwood Washburn** and other anthropologists who engaged with evolutionary theory

## Historical Context

From the architects' point of view, the science that encompassed human evolution and human culture—namely anthropology—had to be included in what was emerging as a new evolutionary cosmology. **The ultimate goal was determining "man's place in nature."**

## Julian Huxley's Evolutionary Humanism

Julian Huxley's "scientific humanism" (later "evolutionary humanism") substituted evolution for conventional religion as a source of knowledge or ethics, grounding his new political philosophy.

## Important Events

### The Unity of Knowledge Conference (1954)

In 1954, an enormous meeting took place that brought an astonishing number of scientists and scholars together:

- Social scientists
- Biologists
- Physical scientists
- Humanists

The conference, titled **"The Unity of Knowledge"**, was held at Columbia University. It included a number of the same architects of the evolutionary synthesis along with prominent anthropologists and psychologists.

### UNESCO Statement on Race (1950)

In 1950, Julian Huxley played an active role in helping to draft the UNESCO statement on race challenging the existence of innate biological differences between different human races.

That statement on race, which included contributors such as anthropologist **Ashley Montagu**, was inspired by a view of race fundamentally shaped by the new evolutionary genetics.

## Significance

The paper documents how the integration of anthropology into evolutionary biology during this period laid the groundwork for modern biological anthropology and shaped secular humanist philosophy grounded in evolutionary science.
